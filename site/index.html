Command Center Integration Monitor

Integration operations teams see their whole trading community, spot changes in traffic sooner and understand what changed.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Daily digest

Generally availableDaily Summarizer and Explainer Agent

Start the day with yesterday's volume, errors, onboarding activity and detected anomalies.

The Command Center Integration Monitor dashboard opens with a digest of the previous day: transaction volume and errors for the top 10 partners, partners onboarded, and anomalies detected.

  • Key analysis: a plain-language explanation of each finding, such as which anomaly monitors saw activity outside their expected pattern.
  • Document summary: top documents for yesterday and last month, with trading community statistics alongside.

The first screen of the day answers what changed overnight, so teams start from the exceptions instead of hunting for them.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Projections and hourly heat map

Generally available

Forecast 7 or 30 days of traffic for any flow, down to the hour.

Command Center Integration Monitor projects expected transaction volume for any saved view of traffic, filtered by direction, document type, document standard and trading partner.

  • Saved projections: keep a projection for each flow that matters, such as purchase orders, ASNs or payments.
  • Projection range: shows the expected low and high for each day, not just a single number.
  • Hourly heat map: shows which hours carry the most traffic, to plan maintenance windows and staffing around them.
  • Anomaly flag: marks any projection with anomalies detected today.

Teams plan around the traffic they expect rather than the traffic they remember, and see at a glance which flows need a closer look today.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Anomaly detection

Generally available

Flag volume spikes and drops against the projected range, hour by hour.

An anomaly monitor can cover all documents, or be scoped to one document type, direction, document standard or trading partner.

  • Marked anomalies: each spike or drop is labeled on the chart with its actual, projected and range values.
  • Anomalies list: every monitor and its detections, by time, in one list.

A spike or a gap in ASN traffic shows up within the hour it happens, while there is still time to find out why.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Failed-transaction anomalies

Generally available

Anomaly monitors watch failed transactions as well as volume.

Failures are measured against an error threshold, and each failed transaction is marked on the timeline with its actual, projected and range values.

A rise in failures on one flow is caught as an anomaly in its own right, rather than hidden inside normal volume.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Transaction volume analysis

Generally available

Break volume down by document type, partner, EDI address, standard and solution ID, right on the dashboard.

Teams can look at 1, 12 or 24 months or a custom range, by day or by week.

  • Refine panel: combine refinements and apply them in one step.
  • Saved filters: keep a refinement and reuse it from the dashboard.
  • Chart choice: line, bar or horizontal bar.

The volume question behind many support calls, such as how much one partner sent this month, is answered on the dashboard without an export.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Trading community

Generally available

Every trading partner in one list, with its status and last activity.

The trading community view lists each partner with its alias, status, BU ID, onboarding date and last activity.

  • Community filters: total partners, partners added last year, last month, this year or this month, and active or inactive partners.
  • Search by identifier: find a partner by name, EDI/ISA ID or BU ID.
  • Export: download the filtered list.

Questions such as which partners were onboarded this quarter, or which have gone quiet, are answered from one list. The same counts appear as trading community statistics on the dashboard, each linking straight to the filtered list.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Network graph

Generally available

See the trading community grouped by transaction volume, and drill into any group.

The graph centers on the client's company, with partners grouped into transaction volume bands. Selecting a spoke opens that band as its own, larger community view with every partner in it; teams can keep drilling in and step back with one click.

  • Volume bands: partners grouped by how much they transact, including a group with no transactions.
  • Community totals: total partners, active partners, EDI addresses and total transactions above the graph.
  • Filters: partner status and communication protocol, such as AS2 or Interconnect.

Where the volume sits and which partners carry it is visible at a glance, and so is the part of the community that is not trading at all.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Partner details

Generally available

One panel for a partner's addresses, document relationships, errors and volume.

Selecting a partner in the trading community opens a details panel covering the whole relationship: EDI addresses and protocol, document relationships by direction, transaction processing errors, transaction volume by EDI address, and the partner's network graph. The same panel opens from Partner integration analysis.

  • Network graph: the partner at the center with its EDI addresses around it, colored by protocol, and totals for EDI addresses, transactions and protocols.
  • Transaction volume: by EDI address over 6, 12 or 24 months.

Investigating a partner starts from one panel instead of several screens, and the same view serves onboarding checks and support calls.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Trading partner stats

Generally available

Track community growth and the share of inactive partners.

A Trading Partners page shows the total community with its active and inactive split, and a trend of total or newly onboarded partners over 1, 3, 6, 12 or 24 months.

Program owners can show how the community has grown, and how much of it is dormant, from the same data the operations team works with.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Document type analysis

Generally available

See which partners are set up for which transactions, and whether each setup is in use.

Document type analysis lays the whole trading community out against every document type and direction, such as 210 inbound, 210 outbound and 214 inbound. Each cell shows whether that partner is configured for that transaction: all configurations active, some configurations inactive, or no configurations.

  • Activity threshold: status is read against a partner activity threshold, so relationships that are configured but idle stand out from those that are trading.
  • Export: download the full view for program reporting.

Clients increasingly use this view to measure community digitization: how many partners exchange each document electronically, where a document such as the 214 carrier status message is configured but inactive, and which partners still need onboarding for a given transaction. A digitization program moves from a spreadsheet exercise to a live view of the community.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Partner integration analysis

Generally available

See how each partner is connected and where it is failing.

Partner integration analysis gives three views of the trading community: document type analysis (above), which partners had transaction processing errors, and which communication protocols partners use.

  • Transaction processing errors: partners ranked by processing errors over the last 30 days.
  • COMMs protocol: partners grouped by protocol, such as SFTP, AS2 and Interconnect, with each group's share of the community.

Onboarding gaps, inactive configurations and protocol concentration become visible without a support request or a spreadsheet export.

Aviator in Command Center Integration Monitor

Command Center Integration Monitor

Ask Aviator

Generally availableCommunity Insights Agent

Ask about the data on screen, from the dashboard to partner integration analysis, and get the answer as a table or a chart.

Aviator is available in Command Center Integration Monitor, loaded with the data behind the page the user is viewing, such as the dashboard or Partner integration analysis. Users can pick a suggested question, such as a breakdown of documents processed by partner, or ask their own.

  • Page context: answers draw on the data already loaded on the page, such as dashboard document volumes or partner configuration and error data.
  • Community questions: such as which document types have had no exchange in over 180 days, by partner and direction, or the most common error types by partner over the last 30 days.
  • Suggested questions: common questions ready to run, and editable before sending.
  • Copy and feedback: every answer has copy and feedback controls.

Ad hoc questions, like which document type moved most last quarter or which relationships have gone quiet, get answered in the flow of work from the client's own Trading Grid data.

Command Center Integration Monitor

Explain with Aviator

Generally availableDaily Summarizer and Explainer Agent

Turn projections and anomalies into written findings, in one click.

Explain with Aviator is an in-app action in Command Center Integration Monitor, not a chat. From Projections and anomalies, Aviator reads the client's own watchlists, projections and anomaly history and returns findings, each labeled by type.

  • Coverage gaps: watchlists that have no anomaly detection configured, and which metrics they leave unmonitored.
  • Related anomalies: anomalies that occurred on the same dates across watchlists, which can point to a shared cause.
  • Working projections: projections that ran with no recorded anomalies.

Teams find the flows they are not monitoring before an outage finds them, and investigate related anomalies once rather than one watchlist at a time.

Trading Grid Insights

Operations and support teams find a transaction in Trading Grid Insights (TG Insights), see exactly what happened to it and act on it, from one place.

Trading Grid Insights

Configurable dashboards

Generally available

Each team opens to its own view of Trading Grid traffic.

Teams create, edit, duplicate and share dashboards built from widgets, and switch between them from the dashboard menu, so an operations lead and a support analyst each open to the view they need. Each dashboard shows when its data was last updated.

  • Volume widgets: documents by trading partner, ranked, for a chosen document type and period, such as invoices over the last seven days.
  • Saved filters: each saved search on the dashboard shows its current count.
  • Exception widgets: critical errors with their processing status, and the alerts that fired recently with severity and result counts.

The first screen of the day shows what is flowing and what is failing, without rebuilding the same search every morning.

Trading Grid Insights

Self-service alerts

Generally available

Turn a saved search into an email alert, without a support request.

Alert rules are built on the same saved filters used in search. Each rule shows its state, the filter it watches and how it is delivered, and the client's own team creates, edits and deletes rules.

  • Saved filters as triggers: alert on the transactions a saved filter returns, such as new purchase orders, errors or shipment status.
  • Templates and acknowledgment rules: start from an alert template, and keep acknowledgment alert rules on their own tab.
  • Email delivery: alerts go out by email subscription, and fired alerts appear in the dashboard's recent alerts widget.

Teams hear about the transactions they care about as they arrive, instead of checking search on a schedule or waiting for a partner to call.

Trading Grid Insights

Self-service reports

Generally available

Set up transaction reports from saved filters, delivered by email.

Report rules work the same way as alert rules. Each names a saved filter, such as all invoices, shipment status or failed purchase orders, and a delivery type, and the reports themselves sit on their own tab next to the rules.

  • Any saved filter: a report covers exactly the transactions a saved search returns.
  • Delivery type: set per rule; the rules shown deliver by email.
  • Managed by the client: create, edit and delete report rules without a support request.

Standing questions, like which purchase orders failed, are answered by a report instead of someone rerunning the same search.

Trading Grid Insights

Transaction search

Generally availableTransaction Search Agent

A Transactions page that answers most questions from the results list.

Each result carries arrival time and status, sender and receiver with their org IDs, OpenText and sender transaction IDs, transaction type and EDI type, and transaction level and correlation ID.

  • Active filters: stay visible beside the results, with a transaction count against each value.
  • Search from transaction: finds a transaction directly by its identifiers.
  • Live updates: a prompt shows when new transactions have arrived, with one-click refresh.

Support analysts spend less time opening individual transactions just to read an ID or a status.

Trading Grid Insights

Facets

Generally available

Narrow transactions by acknowledgment, redelivery, error type and interface.

The Narrow by panel offers facets for delivery and receive acknowledgment status, redelivered and reprocessed status, response status, error type, FA status, and inbound and outbound interface, alongside sender, receiver, processing and delivery status, EDI type, transaction type and transaction level.

A question such as which invoices went out over AS2 and are still waiting on a functional acknowledgment becomes a few clicks in one panel, instead of an export and a spreadsheet.

Trading Grid Insights

Bulk redelivery

Generally available

Resend up to 30 failed transactions in one action.

Users select failed transactions in search results and redeliver them together. A confirmation step lists each selected transaction with its arrival time, processing status and ID before anything is sent.

When a partner endpoint comes back after an outage, the team that finds the failed deliveries can redeliver them straight from search, rather than raising a request for each one. Redelivered status is then available as a facet, so the team can confirm what went back out.

Trading Grid Insights

Reprocess

Generally available

Run a failed file through processing again, straight from search.

Where Redeliver resends a delivery, Reprocess runs the file through processing again. Users select the failed transaction in search and choose Reprocess from the selection toolbar; TG Insights confirms the request, and the row updates when processing completes.

  • One at a time: each request reprocesses a single selected transaction.
  • File level, system errors: offered for file-level transactions whose failure is categorized as a system error, such as ABNORMAL_TERM.
  • Result on the row: the transaction's status icons change from failed to successful once the rerun completes.

A file that failed on a system error is recovered by the team that found it, without a support ticket, and they can see it went through.

Trading Grid Insights

Automated redelivery

Generally availablePowered by Aviator

Aviator matches a failed delivery's error to the client's rules and redelivers it automatically when a rule applies.

Clients configure redelivery rules in TG Insights. When a delivery fails, Aviator matches its error to the configured rules to decide whether it is redelivered automatically, so the rules set exactly what may be retried.

  • Scope: the client's company and address, and the partner address the rule applies to.
  • Conditions: a single document type or any, and an error code or any, with an option to exclude matching values instead.
  • Retry window: how long automatic retries continue, such as 15 minutes.
  • State: each rule is switched on or off, and every rule is listed with its conditions.

Routine retries after a short partner outage no longer wait for someone to notice them, and the client still decides what may be retried.

Trading Grid Insights

Inline transaction details

Generally available

Open a transaction's details in place, without leaving the results list.

Expanding a row shows internal and correlation IDs, sender and receiver, outbound communications, and additional information such as delivery timestamps, filenames, receipts and purge date.

Analysts can compare several transactions side by side in the list, without opening and closing a separate page for each one.

Trading Grid Insights

Transaction detail page

Generally available

One page per transaction, ready to share or export.

Each transaction has its own detail page, with tabs for details, flow, audit log, related transactions and payloads. The page link can be shared with other authorized users, and the page can be exported as a PDF when evidence needs to go outside the organization.

  • Details: internal IDs, sender, receiver, outbound comms and delivery information in one view.
  • Keys: business keys carried with the transaction, such as document and primary IDs.
  • Export as PDF: a record of the transaction to attach to a ticket or send to a trading partner as evidence.

The answer to "what happened to this transaction?" sits on one page that can be passed on as it is.

Trading Grid Insights

Processing Flow

Generally available

See each processing step between sender and receiver, and the time spent at each.

The Flow tab draws a transaction's path through Trading Grid, from the sender through steps such as received, accepted for processing, routed, translated and multi-file processing, to the receiver.

Where a transaction stopped, and which step took longest, is visible at a glance rather than reconstructed from event records.

Trading Grid Insights

Related transactions

Generally available

See the file and document levels of one exchange together.

The Related transactions tab lists every transaction linked to the one being viewed, with arrival time, status, sender, receiver, IDs, transaction type and EDI type.

An analyst can follow a file into the documents it contained, or a document back to its file, without starting a new search.

Trading Grid Insights

Payload viewer

Generally available

Read, search and format transaction content in the browser.

The Payloads tab lists each payload for a transaction, such as its input, output and delivery files, and opens it in a viewer with line numbers that can search the content and format it for reading. Payloads can be downloaded one at a time or several at once.

Checking what was actually sent or received needs no download or separate editor, and the same viewer is where Aviator payload analysis starts.

Trading Grid Insights

Business document viewer

Generally available

Read an EDI document as a business document, or element by element, and export it to PDF.

From a transaction's actions menu in search, the Business document viewer opens the EDI document rendered for reading, such as an X12 810 invoice. Two modes answer different questions:

  • Business mode: the business content only, such as invoice and purchase order numbers, ship-to, terms, and line items laid out as tables with quantity, unit of measure, price and product code.
  • Analyst mode: every segment and element with its ID and code meaning, from the interchange and functional group headers down, such as BIG02 Invoice Number or a REF01 qualifier of DP, Department Number.
  • Export to PDF: share the rendered document outside TG Insights.

Finance and order management teams can answer what an invoice or order said without reading raw EDI, and EDI analysts can check it element by element in the same place.

Trading Grid Insights

Aviator error analysis

Generally availableError Analysis Agent

Explain a delivery failure and the steps to resolve it.

On a failed transaction, the Analyze with Aviator button sends the error code, error message, service and interface details to Aviator. Aviator explains the likely cause, which side of the exchange it sits with, and the steps to resolve it, such as confirming the partner's AS2 endpoint and firewall before the document is reprocessed.

  • Grounded in the transaction: uses the error and delivery details recorded for that transaction.
  • What it checked: lists the signals behind its conclusion, so the analyst can verify before acting.
  • Numbered next steps: who to contact and what to check, in order.
  • Clearly labeled: every analysis carries an AI-generated disclaimer and a feedback control.

Support analysts get from an error code to a next action faster, and newer team members can work failures that would otherwise need an EDI specialist.

Trading Grid Insights

Aviator payload analysis

Generally availablePayload Analysis Agent

Understand a payload in plain language: what it is, what it contains and what is wrong with it.

From the payload viewer, Aviator loads the selected payload and offers ready actions: create a tabulated view, parse the data, or summarize payload details. Each instruction can be edited before it is sent, and follow-up questions stay on that payload.

  • Business summary: the document type and purpose, sender and receiver roles, and key details such as invoice and purchase order numbers and payment terms.
  • Line items as a table: quantities, units, prices and product codes, with totals.
  • Codes explained: qualifiers and codes in plain language, such as a UL qualifier identifying a GS1 Global Location Number.
  • Suggested remediation: when a payload is incomplete or malformed, states what would need to change for the document to process.

What a document says, and whether it was ever complete, is answered in seconds, without reading the raw XML or EDI line by line.

Trading Grid Cartographer

In Trading Grid Cartographer, mapping analysts ask Aviator about their maps in plain language: across the whole map inventory, about a single map, or about how a rule turns source data into target data.

Trading Grid Cartographer

Map inventory intelligence

October 2026Map Inventory Intelligence Agent

Get quick answers about your map inventory.

Finding out which maps exist, who owns them and how much they are used usually means exporting lists or checking map by map. Aviator gives you those answers across the whole inventory in a few seconds, limited to the maps you are allowed to see. That saves time on audits, cleanup and partner questions, and it lets anyone on the team get an answer without waiting for someone who knows where to look.

  • Map status: which maps are active, in testing or retired, and how many fall into each group.
  • Usage: which maps are being used and which have not been used in a long time.
  • Ownership: who owns a map and which maps belong to a given team or person.
  • Recent activity: which maps changed recently and who changed them.
  • Trading partner relationships: which maps are tied to a partner, and which partners use a certain map.
  • Links to the source: each answer links to the map details or a filtered view in Trading Grid Cartographer, so you can open the record and check it.

Questions like “how many EDIFACT maps in production and X12 maps in development” or “show maps that are modified in the last 12 months” are answered in seconds, without exporting lists or searching map by map. Results follow your existing access rights, so you only see maps you already have permission to view.

Trading Grid Cartographer

Map-level intelligence

October 2026Map-level Intelligence Agent

Know a map’s basics without opening every tab.

When you work on a map, you often need a quick fact about it, such as who owns it, when it last changed or which partners it serves. Aviator answers those questions for the map you have selected, so you do not have to open each tab to find them. The answers are short and link back to the source, which makes them quick to read and easy to check.

  • Map details: the map name, owner, status, translator and other basic properties.
  • Map characteristics: for Contivo maps, counts and other measures that describe the size and makeup of the map.
  • Recent activity: when the map last changed and who changed it.
  • Trading partner links: which partners the map is tied to.
  • Links to the source: each answer links to the map details, properties or a filtered view in Trading Grid Cartographer, so you can open the record and check it.

Map details work for any supported translator, and map characteristics are available for Contivo maps only. Questions like “who owns this map”, “how many target fields have no source” or “how many lookups or cross references does this map use” are answered in seconds, without opening each tab and looking for the answer yourself.

Trading Grid Cartographer

Mapping rule explorer

Generally availableMapping Rule Explorer Agent

Understand how a map turns source data into target data, in plain language.

Understanding why a map does what it does can take a long time, especially when the rule was written by someone else. Aviator explains a rule in plain language and shows how a value moves from the source to the target. That helps analysts learn a map faster, find problems sooner and spend less time tracing rules by hand.

  • Rule explanations: what a rule does and why, written the way you would explain it to a coworker.
  • Transformation trace: how a source field becomes a target field, with each step shown in order.
  • Condition checks: how each condition in a rule is evaluated, so you can see which branch applies.
  • Examples: a sample input and the output the rule would produce.
  • Rule snippets: the rule shown as read-only code with a copy button.
  • Links to the source: each answer cites the rule ID and schema path and links to that rule or field in Trading Grid Cartographer, so you can open it and check.

Questions like “why is this field set to a default when the qualifier is BT” or “how does the ship-to part number get its value” are answered in seconds, without opening each rule and tracing it by hand.

Command Center Performance Monitor

Command Center Performance Monitor

Incubation

Aviator agents monitor Trading Grid transactions against rules you set in plain language, explain every finding, and score partner performance.

Built for EDI managers and supply chain, sales and sourcing leaders to measure partner and process performance. Four agents do the monitoring, and you decide what each one watches, using criteria you define:

  • Nora, conformance: validates individual transactions.
  • Lex, timing and SLA: SLA policies across transaction types.
  • Liz, process: business process sequence.
  • Reese, reconciliation: validation across documents.
  • Rules: build one with a wizard, test it against existing transactions, set a budget and deploy it, all in minutes. Rules can use reference data such as carrier codes, a SKU list or SLA grace periods, and show whether each is active, over budget or paused.
  • Findings: triage, assign to a colleague or disposition by priority, with the chain of reasoning behind each finding.
  • Agentic learning: as findings are dispositioned, agents adjust how often and under what conditions they monitor.
  • Dashboard: the top 10 rules by findings, your open actions, and agent metrics for inspections, findings, resolutions and running cost.
  • Analytics: KPIs grouped into order fulfillment, shipping and delivery, invoicing and financial and more, a weighted scorecard you set, and Aviator insights into the root cause of poor performance.
